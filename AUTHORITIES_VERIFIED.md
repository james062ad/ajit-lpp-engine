## Mann v Carnell (1999) 201 CLR 1; [1999] HCA 66

- Verified: 22 Jul 2026, by GJML, from: High Court of Australia,
  official judgment PDF, Mann v Carnell [1999] HCA 66
  (Gleeson CJ, Gaudron, Gummow and Callinan JJ).

- Propositions confirmed against AU-WAIV-01/02, AU-CONF-02:
  CONFIRMED, subject to the qualification noted below:

  1. Waiver occurs where the privilege holder's conduct is inconsistent with
     maintaining the confidentiality that legal professional privilege exists
     to protect. The inquiry is objective; waiver may be imputed even though
     loss of privilege was not subjectively intended.

  2. Disclosure for a limited and specific purpose, including disclosure on
     terms of confidentiality, does not necessarily waive privilege. The
     question remains whether the disclosure was inconsistent with maintaining
     the confidentiality protected by the privilege.

  3. The bare fact of voluntary disclosure to a third party does not, by
     itself, necessarily establish waiver. It is the relevant inconsistency,
     assessed in the circumstances and where necessary informed by
     considerations of fairness, that effects waiver.

- Pinpoints: [28]–[32], particularly [28]–[30]; see also [34]–[35]
  concerning application of the inconsistency test to the disclosure in issue.

- Notes: AU-WAIV-01 and AU-WAIV-02 accurately reflect the majority's
  inconsistency test. AU-CONF-02 should use “does not necessarily waive” or
  “is not of itself sufficient to establish waiver”, rather than “waiver is
  never inferred from the bare fact of disclosure”. The latter formulation is
  unnecessarily absolute and does not reproduce the Court's language precisely.
